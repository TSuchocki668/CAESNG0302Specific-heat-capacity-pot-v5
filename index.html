What happened?

Give equal masses of water and cooking oil the same amount of energy, and the oil's temperature climbs about twice as far. Part of the reason lies in water's hydrogen bond network. As water warms, some of the added energy goes into loosening that network instead of speeding up molecules. Oil molecules have no hydrogen bonds to loosen. Water's restless network helps it resist temperature change, one reason lakes, oceans, and living things warm and cool more gradually than they otherwise would.
